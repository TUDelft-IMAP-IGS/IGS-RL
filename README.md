# Beyond Reward Engineering: Preference-Based Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Criteria Decision Making

This paper presents a novel preference-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework for multi-criteria decision making that moves beyond traditional reward engineering. Rather than relying on manually designed reward functions and weighted objective aggregation, agent behaviour is guided by domain-specific logical heuristics that direct exploration through the decision space. The approach addresses two longstanding challenges in RL: the trial-and-error process of reward design and the preference distortions introduced by reward aggregation in multi-objective settings. Policy selection is performed through IMAP (Integrative Maximisation of Aggregated Preferences), in which decision-maker preferences are specified over complete trajectories and used to evaluate both path quality and end-state outcomes. This enables the derivation of a best-fit policy online while preserving the integrity of the original preference structure, without requiring manually engineered rewards. The framework is demonstrated on a highly sequential maritime logistics scheduling problem characterised by complex operational constraints and competing objectives. Results indicate that the proposed method produces effective and interpretable policies while offering a principled alternative to conventional reward-based reinforcement learning for complex multi-criteria sequential decision problems.
